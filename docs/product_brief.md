# Product brief: Payment Performance & Risk Control Tower

*For a payments squad. Synthetic data: 295,788 payments from 20,000 customers over six months on card, Faster Payments,
Bacs credit and direct debit. Every cost and rate is an assumption in `config/assumptions.yaml`. Generated by
`python -m core.brief`; every figure below comes from a DuckDB query.*

## Problem
Payments teams lose time to the same four questions: why payments fail, where our records disagree with the scheme,
what each rail costs, and how hard to lean on fraud controls. Each is usually answered in a different tool with a
different definition. This project answers all four from one dataset with one metric registry, and ends on a decision.

## Users
Payments product owners (what to fix first), operations and finance (which breaks to work), fraud and risk (where to set
the threshold), and analysts (one definition per metric, SQL always visible).

## Where we stand
Authorisation rate **94.44%**, end-to-end success **94.66%** (retries counted against the original),
cost per settled payment **£0.120**. Reconciliation matches **99.52%** of settled payments; **2,309**
breaks hold **£389,007** at risk (amount mismatch fee 371, amount mismatch unexplained 369, duplicate settlement 385, missing from scheme 592, orphan scheme record 296, unbalanced ledger 296). **795** timing differences were
correctly *not* raised as breaks. Against the planted answer key it found 100% of breaks, classified 100% correctly, and flagged 0 clean payments.

## Four findings
1. **Technical-timeout incident.** From 2026-03-17 10:00 to 12:00 technical declines hit 26.9% of payments against a baseline of 0.77% (52 declines out of 193 payments). This is an incident, not a trend, and is excluded from the trend baseline.  
   *Story:* As a customer paying during an outage, I want my payment to go through or fail fast with a clear message so that I am not left unsure whether I paid.
2. **False-decline hotspot: electronics.** Card payments in electronics are declined at 21.3% against 6.6% for every other category, 3.2 times higher across 15,301 payments. 2,277 of those declines carry the category-rule code, which points to an over-strict rule rather than customer behaviour.  
   *Story:* As a card customer, I want fewer wrongful declines in electronics so that I complete my purchase.
3. **Two settlement clusters on bacs_credit.** bacs_credit settlement time has two clusters, around 50 and 74 hours, with 51% of payments in the slower one. Payments created after 15:00 settle in a median 74 hours against 50 hours before it, so the cut-off adds a day.  
   *Story:* As a business paying suppliers, I want to know before I submit whether my payment misses today's cut-off so that I can plan when funds arrive.
4. **Cost drift: business on card.** Cost per settled payment for business customers on card rose from £0.182 to £0.245, up 34.6%, while the blended average moved only 2.3%. The drift is hidden inside the blended figure.  
   *Story:* As a finance partner, I want cost per payment tracked by segment and rail so that a drift like business card is caught before it shows in the blended average.

## Fraud threshold recommendation
Set the fraud score threshold at 0.355 instead of 0.60. Expected cost falls from £232,728 to £107,648, a saving of £125,080 over six months, while 1.22% of genuine payments are blocked. The choice moves beyond the margin in 3 of six cost scenarios, so treat it as a range. Key assumptions: £12 lost per false decline and the payment amount plus £15 per missed fraud. Before changing anything, ask the fraud and finance owners for confirmed fraud labels with their delay, real margin per payment, and chargeback recovery rates.

Rules that earn their keep on top of the score: **burst of payments in a short window** adds 123 caught frauds for 9 extra blocked customers. The other 2 mostly re-catch what the score already sees and add friction.

## Assumptions to challenge first
1. **Cost inputs (£12 per false decline, amount plus £15 per missed fraud).** The biggest single move is "Fraud loss -50%", which shifts the optimum by +0.065; get real margin, churn and chargeback data.
2. **Fraud labels.** Here they are complete and instant. Real labels arrive weeks late and are incomplete, which biases the curve.
3. **Rail costs.** Cost per payment figures are invented; confirm scheme and processor pricing before acting on the cost drift.

## What I would do first with real data
1. Rebuild the cost curve on confirmed fraud with label dates, and re-run it monthly.
2. Put the reconciliation passes on one real scheme file for a week and compare its queue with the ops team's manual breaks.
3. Agree the metric registry definitions with the squads before building any further dashboards.
